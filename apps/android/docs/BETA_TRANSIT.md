# Transit beta, sharing and local profiles (1.4.0)

Settings → **Beta features** has independent Golden Arrow and MyCiTi switches, both off by default. Map chips control visible trains, Golden Arrow and MyCiTi separately. Disabling an operator cancels its periodic worker and foreground refresh/timetable request. No bus switch enables another operator. Provider instances, timetable parsers, source checks and cache keys are separate.

## Source data and freshness

| Service | Timetables | Map | Fares |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trains | Existing CT Trains query/date/time cache and native status2 alerts | Existing Wikidata positions and OSM rail trace | Existing published fares and personal corrections |
| MyCiTi | `var timetable` on the operator’s [route pages](https://www.myciti.org.za/en/timetables/route-timetables/), exact route ID and selected weekday; `static_time` preserved | `map_routes`, `map_stops`, `map_shapes` from the [operator map](https://www.myciti.org.za/en/routes-stops/route-stop-station-map/); only published ID/name/position/route fields imported | Published [pay-as-you-go table](https://www.myciti.org.za/en/myconnect-fares/pay-as-you-go/), with explicitly identified payment product and fare band |
| Golden Arrow | [Operator catalogue](https://www.gabs.co.za/Timetable.aspx), anonymous ASP.NET postback/cookies, linked PDFs; effective dates per page, timetable number, weekdays and footnotes | Western Cape [Transportation MapServer](https://gis.westerncape.gov.za/server2/rest/services/SpatialDataWarehouse/Transportation/MapServer), surveyed layers 5/6, paginated in WGS84 | [Operator product table](https://www.gabs.co.za/ProductDetails.aspx), original Five Ride/Weekly/Monthly product units and transfer rules |

Golden Arrow survey capture dates are not supplied. These stops/routes are explicitly **unverified for current operation**. Survey route codes are not timetable numbers: the app does not fuzzy-match them or invent interchange stops. Untimed `via` points are omitted from timed calls. PDF column clocks determine the order of timed points because publisher row order can differ from trip order. Unknown annotations and ambiguous cross-midnight columns are rejected. A source PDF remains available through its published link when extraction is unsupported. Timetable patterns do not establish holiday exceptions, vehicle punctuality or platform walking directions.

Default bus refresh: 60 minutes; selectable 15–240 minutes. Android WorkManager requires connectivity and may defer runs for battery/OS reasons. Saved bus favourites are refreshed separately (up to five per worker). Golden Arrow survey geometry is reused for 24 hours; timetable catalogue/fares have separate check dates. HTTP failures retain available components and their old dates. The UI shows partial-refresh notes and cached timetable status. No failed refresh becomes a “normal service” report. Bus caches are published weekday patterns, unlike the date-specific train search cache. Confirm their effective dates before travel.

Map distance is never treated as a fare distance. A weekly product is never divided to invent a cash fare. Personal corrections are keyed by operator, fare row and product, labelled separately and removable. The budget multiplies quantities of the selected **product**.

## Map views and personal spots

MapLibre uses OpenFreeMap Liberty/Streets, Positron/Light and Dark. No Google Maps SDK, Maps API key or billing account. Dark is offered by the provider but described by its maintainers as unfinished. City of Cape Town **January 2025 aerial orthophotography** is rendered via public raster exports. Provider attribution and imagery year remain visible. It shows photographed buildings/roads, not a live satellite feed.

Imagery metadata: [2025 aerial service](https://cityimg.capetown.gov.za/erdas-iws/esri/GeoSpatial%20Datasets/rest/services/Aerial%20Imagery_Aerial%20Imagery%202025Jan/MapServer). The stated digital-file permission is for **non-commercial purposes**. Obtain appropriate rights before changing this personal/community app to commercial distribution. Native download packs record their actual style; selecting another online style does not relabel an old offline pack. Existing 3-region / 200 MB / 5,000-tile download bounds remain. Map downloads are public cache, managed separately from profile backups.

With permission, the map samples foreground location and suggests a station only within 1.5 km and at reported accuracy ≤500 m. The label says “closest”, not that the user definitely boarded or is at a platform. Saved route endpoints and favourites supply quick destination chips. Explicit place searches use OpenStreetMap Nominatim, restricted to the Cape Town region, with no autocomplete and a request delay. Search results can be reviewed and marked; they are not official transit stops.

Hold the map to drop a personal pin, or use place search. Choose a service/tag/note and optional photo from a picker or camera app. Pins and photos remain local; photos are downsampled, re-encoded without camera/GPS metadata and Keystore-encrypted. They do not modify the operator dataset or upload to a city-wide database.

## Portable files and profiles

Tools → **Safety trips** builds a reviewed `.cttrip` JSON itinerary from a selected train journey or bus pattern: leave from building, estimated access time and buffer, boarding, transfer(s), alighting and final walk. Source-provided platform numbers are preserved; unknown values stay unknown. The user chooses bus travel date, matching weekday and effective dates. No platform or walking duration is inferred. Android’s file share sheet can use Quick Share/Nearby Share, WhatsApp or other installed apps. Formatted text sharing is also available.

OpenDocument and content-URI VIEW/SEND imports support `.cttrip`, `.ctreport` and `.ctcircle`. Import previews precede saving. Unknown formats/versions/fields, excessive nesting/size, invalid coordinates and malformed trip steps are rejected. Importing never starts GPS, joins a room, enables notifications, schedules a reminder or posts a message.

The optional local profile name labels files. It is not an online identity or multi-user server account. A password-encrypted `.ctprofile` file can be saved through Android’s document picker, outside app-private storage. AES-256-GCM and PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 (210,000 iterations, random salt/nonce) make it independent of the installation’s Keystore. A linked file can receive automatic updates as local data changes while the app process is active. If access fails, automatic saving pauses and asks the user to select a writable file again. SAF providers vary; keep a separate backup copy for important records.

Backups include personal preferences, journeys, itinerary files, pin photos, cached train queries and bus data. They exclude Matrix/Google sessions, the auto-backup password and active live-trip capabilities/positions. Pending observations become local records. Restore previews replacement; reminders are stopped, hosted sharing disabled, report counts treated as local observations, and native map-region IDs cleared. Validated photos get fresh identifiers so a failed database transaction cannot overwrite current profile photos. Existing anonymous storage is retained; creating a name is optional.

**Uninstall removes private Room/Keystore storage.** Only the user-selected external file can survive. Android revokes the old document grant on uninstall, so reinstall requires selecting the backup again and entering its password. The app does not auto-scan private folders or claim that a raw encrypted Room copy can restore a deleted Keystore key.

## Community reports and hosted chat

Reports are structured service/category/stop/route/direction/observation-time/message records. They can be drafted and saved offline, shared as consistently formatted text or imported files. A delay word in a chat draft offers a structured report form; it does not infer the stop, route or operational status. Official alerts and community observations stay separate.

Optional hosted communication uses the [Matrix Client-Server API](https://spec.matrix.org/latest/client-server-api/) and a user-chosen HTTPS homeserver, such as public Matrix hosting. It requires no infrastructure operated by the app owner. Account creation and server SSO can be done in a trusted Matrix client; this native client supports password login where offered or a token from that trusted client. Passwords are not saved; tokens are Keystore-encrypted, sent only in Authorization headers, with redirects disabled. Disconnect clears local credentials and attempts server logout.

Each service has its own chosen room. Users explicitly join/create/share its room ID and explicitly send messages/reports. Community rooms are unencrypted and may be readable by members and hosting providers. Matrix-encrypted rooms are rejected because this small client does not implement Olm/Megolm; use a full Matrix client such as Element for those rooms.

Confirmed queued posts use idempotent transaction IDs and the same account/room. WorkManager retries with backoff; observations older than one hour or queued for a different account/room are dropped. The UI’s report count means **distinct Matrix accounts**, deduplicated by account, service, stop, route and direction using each account’s newest category. It does not mean verified humans, a city-wide database or an official delay. Local/imported/restored reports do not increase counts. Room moderation, abuse controls and federated provider policies remain external constraints.

## Optional live trips

A separate private, invite-only Matrix room is created for one reviewed safety trip. The owner invites a Matrix friend and explicitly shares a `.ctcircle` capability file containing the room, owner identity, itinerary and random trip key. The invitation expires after two hours. The recipient reviews/imports it, then explicitly starts watching. The owner separately grants location permission and starts sharing. A persistent foreground notification has Stop; there is no boot receiver, silent restart or background-location permission.

GPS/check-ins are sampled roughly once a minute while the foreground service runs, for at most two hours. Android/network restrictions can interrupt or defer updates; this is not a guaranteed safety monitoring service. Offline positions are not queued. Manual Leaving / At boarding stop / On board / At transfer / Arrived states are explicit observations; proximity alone never proves boarding. Friends can receive phase-change notifications with notification permission. Updates older than five minutes disappear from the map and are labelled unknown; owner location is not written as a location history. Friend coordinates also remain in memory only and are discarded on stop/expiry; encrypted check-in/replay metadata may remain locally.

Trip payloads use app-specific AES-256-GCM encryption, bound to circle/room/owner, with sender, expiry, timestamp and replay-sequence checks. The homeserver still sees room membership and message timing. This is **not standard Matrix E2EE**, does not provide forward secrecy/key verification, and has not been independently audited. Anyone receiving the capability can read that trip’s updates until expiry. Keep it private; stopping does not retract already shared files or messages. Do not promote it as a production Life360 replacement without two-device testing and security review.

## Verification

Core tests cover sanitized factual publisher fixtures, operator isolation, latitude/longitude order, route/day mismatches, Golden Arrow footnotes/effective dates, untimed points, survey-code mismatch, published product units, accuracy bounds, strict imports, portable crypto/tampering, live sender/room/key/replay/staleness/expiry checks, distinct account counts and Google token signature/claims. Debug/release compilation and lint verify Android integration. API 35 instrumentation tests exercise native large-record storage, actual PDF extraction, encrypted photo backup/restore and independent operator switches; the release workflow requires that job to pass. Real camera/document-provider grants, MapLibre offline downloads, Google consent and two-account Matrix live sharing still require device testing. A successful build is not evidence that those external account/provider flows have been exercised.
